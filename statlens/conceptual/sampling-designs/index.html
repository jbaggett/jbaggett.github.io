Sampling Designs

Every design here chooses at random in some way — except the last one. The question is not whether randomness is used, but what it is applied to.

  • Simple random — everything equally likely. Fair, and scattered across the whole site, so you cover a lot of ground.
  • Stratified — split the site into its natural bands first, then sample each at random in proportion to its size. Guarantees every band is represented.
  • Cluster — pick a few whole groups at random and take everything in them. Far less travelling.
  • Multistage — pick a few groups, then a random sample within each. Less travelling and less measuring.
  • Convenience — take whatever is easy to reach. No randomness at all.

Strata and clusters are opposites

A stratum is a group you know about in advance and want to control: good ones are uniform inside and different from each other. A cluster is a group that is cheap to collect whole — and you do not choose what is inside it, you get whatever the site's convenient units happen to contain.

That is why the setting matters. On the beach you can walk a transect from the water up (crossing every zone) or toss a quadrat (all one zone); ecologists do both. In the orchard a row crosses all three varieties while a tree is one variety. Underground there is only the hole: a patch at depth would mean excavating everything above it and throwing the spoil away, which is the opposite of convenient.

Watch how much you had to collect next to how close you got. That trade — precision against effort — is the whole reason these designs exist.
