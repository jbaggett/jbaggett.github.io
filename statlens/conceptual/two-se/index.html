Why ± 2 SE Works — Help

This figure makes the argument behind every statistic ± 2 SE confidence interval, without a single number in it.

  • The curve is the sampling distribution of the statistic. Its centre is the parameter and its spread is one standard error, so the axis can be marked off symbolically: p−2SE, p−SE, p, p+SE, p+2SE.
  • The shaded band is the middle of that distribution — by the empirical rule, about 68% within 1 SE, 95% within 2 SE, 99.7% within 3 SE.
  • The marker is one sample's statistic. Drag it, use the slider (arrow keys work), or press Draw a sample to let the computer pick one from the curve.
  • The bar under the axis is that sample's interval, reaching 2 SE either side. It is drawn in blue when it reaches the parameter and in orange when it does not.

The argument. The interval reaches the parameter exactly when the statistic landed within 2 SE of it — the distance between them is the same distance either way. So the interval captures the parameter for exactly the samples in the shaded band, which is about 95% of them. That is all “95% confident” claims.

What it does not say. For any one interval the parameter is either in it or not. The 95% is the success rate of the method across samples, not a probability about the interval in front of you.

In practice SE is not known — it is estimated, from a bootstrap distribution or from a formula. That is the one gap between this picture and the interval a student actually computes, and it is why the real coverage is a little under the advertised rate at small n. CI Coverage measures that; Why the Percentile CI Works makes the matching argument for percentile intervals.
