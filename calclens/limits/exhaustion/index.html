A circle of radius 1 has area π. Trap it between a polygon drawn inside and a polygon drawn outside, then add sides. Neither polygon is a circle, but together they leave the circle nowhere to hide — which is how Archimedes found π, and it is the squeeze theorem two thousand years early.

Why this is the squeeze theorem

Two sequences with the same limit, and the thing you care about trapped between them. That is the whole hypothesis, and Archimedes was using it in the third century BC — with 96 sides, by hand, no decimal notation and no trigonometry tables, to get .

A footnote for accuracy: he squeezed the circumference between the two perimeters, not the area between the two areas. Both work, and both give π. The area version is the one shown here because it is the one that leads to sin(x)/x below.
