Why nothing ever equals 0/0

Substituting h = 0 into the quotient gives 0/0, and you will see that said in the margin — never on a line. 0/0 is not a number the limit equals. It is the result of an attempt that failed, which is why the next move is to rewrite. Writing "" is the single most common way to lose marks on this topic, so this page never models it.

For the same reason stays on every line until the limit is actually taken. Dropping it early is the same false equality in a different place.

The three moves

  • Polynomial — multiply out, collect, factor h, cancel. Everything without an h in it cancels; that is what makes the rest divisible by h.
  • Rational — common denominator first, then the same thing.
  • Root — multiply top and bottom by the conjugate, which clears the root by (a − b)(a + b) = a² − b².

Cancelling h is legal because the limit never uses h = 0 — it only uses values near 0, and at every one of those h/h = 1. That sentence is the whole justification, and it is on the line that does the cancelling.

What it will not do

sin, cos, ln and eˣ are declined, not guessed at: their difference quotients need a special limit such as rather than algebra. The page says so and hands you the derivative from the rules instead. A tool that confidently stated a wrong limit would be worse than no tool.

To see the rules applied instead, use Worked Derivative.
