Secant to Tangent

P stays put. Q slides along the curve toward it. The line through P and Q is a secant, and its slope is the difference quotient — the average rate of change between the two points.

As h shrinks, the secant pivots. If it settles on one particular line, that line is the tangent and its slope is the derivative .

What to watch for

  • The table converges from both sides. Switch sides and check that the two columns of numbers head for the same place — if they do not, there is no derivative there.
  • Try |x| with P at 0. From the right the slopes are all +1, from the left all −1. They never agree, so no tangent exists — a corner, not a smooth point.
  • The falling ball preset is the classic: height −16t²+32t+48 with P at t = 0.5. Average velocities over shorter and shorter intervals pile up at 16 ft/sec, which is the speedometer reading at that instant.
  • Leave Show the tangent off until students have called out the number they think the slopes are heading for.

Putting this in a slide or a page

Every control is URL-addressable, so a lecture slide can link a stripped-down version. For example:

?f=-16x^2%2B32x%2B48&var=t&a=0.5&window=0,2
   &embed=true&controls=h&tangent=false

gives just the figure, the h slider and the table, with no function box and no tangent revealed. See docs/url-api.md for every parameter.
