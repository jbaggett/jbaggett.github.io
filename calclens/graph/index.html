Grapher

Type a function and it is drawn. Type another and it joins it. Anything that is not the variable becomes a slider.

What it does that a plain grapher does not

  • f′ and f″ are differentiated symbolically, not estimated — so they are exact, and they are drawn with their own dash patterns rather than relying on colour.
  • Critical and inflection points are found as roots of f′ and f″ and marked on the curve. Points where the function only touches zero without crossing are not found; nothing is invented.
  • Vertical asymptotes are detected and drawn, and the curve is never joined across one. A grapher that connects the two branches of 1/x is drawing a line that is not there.
  • Horizontal asymptotes are drawn too, and the two ends are reported separately because they can differ: 3x/√(x²+1) approaches 3 on the right and −3 on the left. They are measured by running x out to ±10⁸ and checking that the values settle, so a limit that converges very slowly — 1/ln x — is not claimed.
  • Slant asymptotes as well, by the same measurement: the slope is what f(x)/x settles at, and the intercept is what is left once that slope is taken away. A line is not dashed as its own asymptote — true, and it annotates nothing.
  • A tangent line at a point you can slide.

Embedding it

Everything is in the URL, so a graph can be dropped into a page, a quiz or a homework problem as an iframe:

<iframe src="https://learnlens.org/calclens/graph/?f=x%5E3-3x&window=-3,3&plot=only"
        width="600" height="400" style="border:0"></iframe>

plot=only renders the figure alone — no controls, no chrome. Leave it off and the reader gets the sliders too, which is what you want for a demo rather than a static figure. The page reports its own height to the framing document, so the iframe can size itself.

Giving a class fewer things to switch on

layers= decides which toggles the reader is offered. It is a different axis from show=, which decides what is on. A class meeting f′ for the first time gets

?f=x%5E3-3x&layers=deriv

— one checkbox and nothing else to wander into. layers=none offers nothing at all, and leaving it off offers everything. The two combine: show=deriv&layers=none puts the derivative on screen without letting anyone switch it off.
