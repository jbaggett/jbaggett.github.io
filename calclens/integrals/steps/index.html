    Worked Integral

    Type any integrand of one variable. The page antidifferentiates it the way you would on paper: one rule per line, with the rule named beside the line that used it.

    Where the constant comes from

    An integral is not equal to an antiderivative. It is equal to an antiderivative plus a constant, so and the + C appears on the exact line where the last integral sign disappears — not at the end as an afterthought. Lines that still carry an integral sign never show it, because the constant has not been earned yet.

    Substitution is a choice, so it is written down

    The page could integrate by looking up cosine and dividing by 3. That is the move students learn as a reflex and then misapply to , where dividing by 2x is not allowed. So nothing composite is looked up: the substitution is named, du is worked out, and the integral is rewritten in u before anything is integrated.

    Each substitution line says what u is and what made it the one to try — the rest of the integrand being exactly du. The x goes back in as its own labelled line at the end, because forgetting that step is how a correct substitution still earns no marks.

    How far it goes

    Up to and including substitution: the table, sums, constant multiples, multiplying out, and a change of variable. That is the Calculus 1 toolkit.

    Beyond it, the page says which technique the integral needs — integration by parts, partial fractions, trigonometric substitution — and stops. Those are Calculus 2. If you get one of those messages your setup was most likely fine; the obstacle is a method you have not met yet.

    Some integrands, among them, have no antiderivative in elementary terms at all. No technique will find one, and the page says so rather than guessing.

    If your answer looks different

    Two antiderivatives of the same function can differ by a constant and by a great deal of algebra, so a correct answer need not match the last line here. Check My Answer compares two answers numerically and will settle it.

    Putting this in a slide or a page

    ?f=2x cos(x^2)&embed=true&steps=all&controls=none

    gives the finished working with no input box. Leave steps off and the class advances it a line at a time. See docs/url-api.md for every parameter.
