Accumulation Function

The top graph is a function f. The bottom graph records the signed area collected under f, starting at a and sweeping right to x. That running total is a new function of x, written .

What to watch for

  • Where f is above the axis, area is added and A rises. Where f is below, area is subtracted and A falls.
  • Where f crosses zero, A stops rising or falling and turns around. That is a maximum or minimum of A — not of f.
  • The steepness of A at x equals the height of f at x. Turn on "Show the slope link" and compare the two marked values as you sweep. That is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 1.
  • A(a) = 0 always — nothing has been collected yet.

Try this

  1. With f(x) = 2x − 2 and a = 0, sweep from 0 to 3. A dips, bottoms out at x = 1, then climbs. Ask why the low point is exactly where f crosses the axis.
  2. Move a from 0 to 1. The whole A graph shifts vertically but its shape does not change — every antiderivative differs by a constant.
  3. Try f(x) = e−x². There is no formula for its antiderivative, yet A is a perfectly ordinary curve. The accumulation function exists whether or not anyone can write it down.
