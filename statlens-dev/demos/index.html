Teaching DemosStatLens

Built for the front of a room rather than for a student working alone: one question, one obvious action, a source small enough to check by hand, and a pile that grows while you talk. No dataset menus, no settings.

Every demo has two links — the bare page for lecture, and a guided version that adds the questions through an activity file. Ask them yourself in class; the guided link is for students working alone.

  • Where does X² come from?

    Throw 50 marks at four blocks of colour whose areas are the claimed shares, read X² off a four-row table, and repeat. Dots become bars, bars grow a hump, and at 500 samples the χ²(3) curve settles over the pile. A scrubber goes back to any earlier sample. After a demo by Todd Will.

More demos are planned — what a shuffle shows, whether another sample would agree, where the bell curve comes from, where F comes from. They share one stage, so each new one is a configuration rather than a new tool.
