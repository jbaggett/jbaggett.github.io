Where does X² come from?

Each sample throws 50 independent marks at four blocks of colour whose areas are the claimed shares — 32%, 32%, 20%, 16%. Count how many land on each colour, compare with the 16, 16, 10, 8 you expected, and add up (O−E)²/E. That total is X². Repeat, and the X² values build a distribution.

X² is the statistic you compute from one sample. χ² is the distribution those statistics tend towards — the dashed curve that appears once there are 500 samples.

Add ?observed=on to mark a real sample (14 blue, 14 amber, 16 green, 6 mauve — X² = 4.60) on the pile and shade everything at least that far out.
