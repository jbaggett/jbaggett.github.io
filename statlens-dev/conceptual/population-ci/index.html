Confidence Intervals by Repeated Sampling — Help

The Sampling Distribution Lab's three tiers — population, one sample, the distribution of the statistic — with one addition: every sample gets a confidence interval, and every dot is coloured by whether that interval caught μ.

  • Each interval is the one you would actually compute: x ± t*s/√n, using that sample's own s. So the widths differ from dot to dot, and the coverage lands near the level you asked for rather than exactly on it.
  • Blue dots caught μ; orange ones missed. Mostly the misses are far from μ — mostly, because s is estimated too, so an unluckily small s can produce a narrow interval that misses from close by.
  • Hover a dot to see that sample in the One sample tier, with its interval drawn under the axis.
  • Change the confidence level and the same samples are re-measured: wider intervals, more hits. The simulation does not re-run, so what you are watching is the price of confidence.

“But I only get one sample.” Quite. Press Hide μ: the colours vanish and the count disappears, because without μ nothing can tell which intervals worked — not the page, and not you. That is the real situation. Press Reveal and they snap back: your interval was one colour or the other all along, and hiding μ never changed which. It only removed your ability to see it, which is why the 95% has to be a claim about the method rather than about your interval.
